# Wayfinder Constitution

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** CANONICAL

The Constitution defines rules that implementation, intelligence, projections, and user experience must not violate without an explicit Canon change.

## 1. Reality before interpretation

A factual record and a claim about its meaning are different objects.

- `Workout occurred` is factual.
- `Training consistency is improving` is derived.
- `I feel proud of my consistency` is authored reflection.

These must never be silently collapsed.

## 2. Unknown is not zero

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

No meal log does not mean zero calories. No relationship log does not mean no interaction. No recorded work does not mean no work occurred.

Unknown must remain representable throughout storage, derivation, reasoning, and UI.

## 3. Planned is not happened

Intent, schedule, plan, action, and completed event are distinct.

The passage of time cannot transform a planned action into a factual event.

## 4. AI is non-authoritative over canonical reality

AI may read, compare, reason, hypothesize, summarize, explain, and propose. It may not silently establish that something happened.

Canonical writes must pass through an authorized command path with domain validation.

## 5. Permanent growth requires evidence

Wayfinder may not grant permanent growth merely because a user clicked a button, accepted a suggestion, created a goal, or scheduled an action.

Growth must descend from qualifying evidence defined by the owning domain and/or an accepted derivation rule.

## 6. Derived state is reconstructable

Character, momentum, bearing, patterns, mastery, and similar read models are projections over deeper records.

Deleting a projection must not delete lived history.

## 7. Meaning remains distinct from observation

User-authored meaning, system-generated interpretation, and tentative hypothesis are epistemically different.

Wayfinder must preserve authorship and certainty.

## 8. Important conclusions expose lineage

The system should be able to answer: `Why do you think that?`

Important derived claims should trace to supporting evidence, sources, rules, and versions where practical.

## 9. Domains own their reality

Training owns training truth. Finance owns financial truth. Relationships own relationship truth.

The core provides contracts, references, provenance, composition, evidence, direction, and reasoning seams. It does not force all domains into one universal persistence shape.

## 10. The model is not the person

Wayfinder's representation may be incomplete, stale, uncertain, or wrong.

The architecture must preserve the ability to express uncertainty, correction, disagreement, and missing context.

## 11. Cross-domain interaction requires explicit seams

One domain must not directly mutate another domain's factual state.

Cross-domain effects occur through stable references, accepted events, commands, evidence links, or explicitly designed contracts.

## 12. Authority and intelligence remain separate

Reasoning quality must not determine write authority.

A better model may make better proposals, but the mechanism that changes canonical state remains governed by deterministic contracts and permissions.

## 13. Ontology should stay smaller than the life it models

New primitives require justification. Prefer domain-specific concepts unless multiple domains truly depend on a shared distinction.

The system must resist both extremes: everything-is-an-event under-modeling and abstract-object explosion.

## 14. Preserve history; evolve interpretation

Changing a rule should not rewrite what happened.

Where interpretation or rewards depend on historical rules, version the derivation or freeze the relevant snapshot so history remains explainable.

## 15. Build through complete vertical slices

Wayfinder should become useful early.

Prefer one complete reality-to-evidence-to-projection loop over many half-built subsystems.
